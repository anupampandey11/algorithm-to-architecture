❓ WHAT is happening?
We compare the boundary bars at Left and Right pointers. The shorter bar establishes the active bottleneck.
🧠 WHY this decision? (The Two-Pointer Invariant)
Water level at any position is bounded by min(maxLeft, maxRight). Because height[left] <= height[right], we know with 100% mathematical certainty that the right side has a wall of at least height[right] >= leftMax. Thus, leftMax is the sole limiting factor!
📍 WHERE is water trapped?
Evaluating current pointer position. If pillar height is less than the boundary maximum, water fills the gap.
⏱️ WHEN do we advance pointers?
Advance the pointer on the shorter side inward (left++ or right--) because its trapped water is now fully computed and cannot be affected by any unseen inner pillars.
📐 Mathematical Computation:
Trapped Water = max(0, min(leftMax, rightMax) - height[i])
⚡ C++ Two-Pointer Algorithm
O(N) Time • O(1) Space

12 } else {

14 rightMax = height[right];
15 else
16 total += rightMax - height[right];
17 right--;
18 }
19 }

🧠 The Trapping Rain Water Invariant Explained

At any column i, the trapped water is determined by the height of the shortest boundary bounding it from both sides:

Water[i] = max(0, min(max_left[i], max_right[i]) - height[i])
💡 The Two-Pointer Breakthrough (From O(N) Space to O(1) Space):
Normally, calculating max_left and max_right requires two auxiliary arrays ($O(N)$ extra memory).

However, notice that if height[left] <= height[right]:
  • We already know the maximum bar seen so far on the left is leftMax.
  • We know that on the right side, there exists at least one bar with height >= height[right] >= height[left].
  • Therefore, leftMax is guaranteed to be smaller than or equal to the actual global right maximum!
  • This means min(max_left, max_right) = leftMax without needing to inspect any of the unseen bars in the middle!

This rigorous mathematical property allows us to safely calculate the water at left and increment left++ (or conversely at right and decrement right--) in a single linear pass $O(N)$ with $O(1)$ auxiliary variables.
